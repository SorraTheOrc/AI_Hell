## 1. Audio Vision Statement

AI Hell sounds like a machine that is enjoying itself. The sonic world of the
game is built on two fused impulses: the cold, geometric tension of classic
arcade space shooters — the sense of a screen full of hostile geometry closing
in — and the relentless, escalating survival energy of modern horde games,
where the pressure never quite relents and the player's own fire is the
metronome. Every layer of music must feel like it is being generated by the
same rogue intelligence that builds the enemy waves: precise, patterned,
slightly wrong, and utterly unshakeable.

The musical identity is unapologetically synthetic and motif-driven. The
palette is FM synthesis (two to four operators) and chiptune waveforms —
square, triangle and noise — the sound of hardware that never existed but that
every player recognises instantly. Melody carries the identity: AI Hell's
motifs are deliberately short (two to six notes) so they can be looped,
fragmented, recombined and layered without ever becoming a tune the player
tires of. Density, not melody change, is the primary expressive tool. When the
waves get harder the music does not switch to a different piece; it grows more
voices, faster arpeggios, harder bass and more noise.

The emotional arc of a run is a single unbroken ramp with four stations:
**arrival** in the menu starfield, where the ambient Lydian material floats and
promises scale; **engagement** as the first wave spawns and the Phrygian
Dominant combat material takes over; **escalation** as lives dwindle, waves
overlap and the score climbs; and **resolution** at the boss kill or the
player's death, where the score finally stops pushing and lets the outcome
land. The score is a co-conspirator, not a narrator: it never tells the player
what to feel, it applies pressure and then releases it.

Because AI Hell is a game about rhythm as much as aim, the music is welded to
the same clock as the gameplay. Player fire is quantised to a **silent internal
80 BPM beat** (GDD §4.4), and explosion accents are deferred to the next 16th
note of that grid. Every tempo zone in this chapter is an integer multiple or
division of 80 BPM, so a future soundtrack can lock to the very grid the
player's weapons already obey.

## 2. Musical Identity

### 2.1 Tonal Language

The soundtrack is built around two complementary scales. They are not
interchangeable: one is the voice of combat, the other the voice of the space
between combats, and the transition between them is a composed event (see
§5).

**Primary Combat Scale — Phrygian Dominant**

- **Used for:** wave combat, boss spawns, high-intensity survival phases, the
  ESCALATION and BOSS mix states.
- **Scale formula:** 1 ♭2 3 4 5 ♭6 ♭7
- **Key centre:** E — **E – F – G♯ – A – B – C – D**
- **Why:** retro-arcade tension; exotic, driving and instantly "space danger";
  works extremely well with FM bass, square leads and arpeggiators; provides
  strong melodic hooks with minimal notes.

**Secondary Ambient Scale — Lydian**

- **Used for:** menus, starfield travel, upgrade and mineral-choice screens,
  low-intensity exploration, the AMBIENT mix state.
- **Scale formula:** 1 2 3 ♯4 5 6 7
- **Key centre:** C — **C – D – E – F♯ – G – A – B**
- **Why:** bright, floating and cosmic; contrasts the combat scale without
  breaking aesthetic cohesion; ideal for pads, shimmering arpeggios and soft
  synth textures.

#### Key Centre Reference

| Element | Combat | Ambient |
|---------|--------|---------|
| **Scale** | Phrygian Dominant | Lydian |
| **Key centre** | E | C |
| **Pitches** | E – F – G♯ – A – B – C – D | C – D – E – F♯ – G – A – B |
| **Characteristic degree** | ♭2 (F) — the "wrong" note that signals danger | ♯4 (F♯) — the raised fourth that signals lift and space |
| **Registered flag** | `music/state/engaged` | `music/state/ambient` |

All harmonic content is conceived relative to these two key centres. The two
scales share five of seven pitches (A, B, C, D, E); the only points of
difference are F♯/F and G/G♯. That shared core is the designed pivot between
ambient and combat material: a transition may pass through the common tones,
but **a texture must never sound F♯ against F, or G against G♯, at the same
moment.** Composers should treat the two scales as two faces of one harmonic
system rather than as separate keys.

#### Mode Discipline

- Phrygian Dominant is the **active** mode: it may sustain for a whole wave,
  a boss phase or an entire level.
- Lydian is the **rest** mode: it owns menus, interstitial screens and the
  post-wave lull, and it must not carry combat intensity.
- Neither mode is a reward mode. AI Hell does not "resolve to major" at the
  end of a run — victory and defeat are both resolved inside the established
  language (see §2.2 and §5.3), so that a win never sounds like a different
  game and a loss never sounds like a joke.

### 2.2 Core Motifs

Motifs are intentionally short (2–6 notes) to support looping, procedural
recombination and chiptune-style repetition. Each motif is an **atomic musical
object** — an interval set plus a rhythm cell — and must be stored that way in
any tooling so fragments can be recombined without re-authoring (see §6.2).

**Combat Motif A — "Incoming Wave"**

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| **Scale** | Phrygian Dominant (E) |
| **Contour** | Rising tension → abrupt drop |
| **Notes** | **E – F – G♯ – F – E – D** |
| **Usage** | Triggered at the start of each enemy wave; can be fragmented into E–F–G♯ for micro-stingers; works well as a three-note arpeggiated alert. |

**Combat Motif B — "Survival Pulse"**

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| **Scale** | Phrygian Dominant (E) |
| **Rhythmic cell** | Dotted + straight |
| **Notes** | **G♯ – A – G♯ – F – E** |
| **Usage** | Mid-combat loop; easily reharmonised for intensity scaling; can be doubled with FM bass for crunchy retro drive. |

**Ambient Motif — "Star Drift"**

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| **Scale** | Lydian (C) |
| **Contour** | Floating, circular |
| **Notes** | **C – E – F♯ – E – D – C** |
| **Usage** | Menu screens; upgrade and mineral-choice interface; slow arpeggiated background texture. |

#### Motif Reference Table

| Motif | Pitch content | Emotional trigger | Permitted usage | Forbidden usage | Optional usage |
|-------|---------------|-------------------|-----------------|-----------------|----------------|
| **Incoming Wave** (Combat A) | E – F – G♯ – F – E – D | Arrival, threat, "here they come"; the start of a wave | Wave-start stinger; ENGAGED loop statement; ESCALATION fragmentation; menu attract loop as a distant echo | Not in AMBIENT as a complete statement; never harmonised outside Phrygian Dominant; never over the boss intro | Three-note fragment E–F–G♯ as an arpeggiated alert; retrograde as a bass figure under ESCALATION |
| **Survival Pulse** (Combat B) | G♯ – A – G♯ – F – E | Endurance, momentum, the grind of survival; the player's own persistence | Mid-combat loop; ESCALATION crescendo; boss-phase bed | Not in AMBIENT or RESOLVE; not as a stinger on its own | Doubled on FM bass; rhythmically augmented (half-time) for the ESCALATION crawl before the boss |
| **Star Drift** (Ambient) | C – E – F♯ – E – D – C | Calm, drift, scale, the pause between runs | Menu and attract mode; upgrade and mineral-choice screens; post-wave lull | Not during wave combat; not overlaid on Phrygian Dominant material; not used for the death or victory resolution | Slow arpeggiated texture on triangle wave; inverted form as a shimmering pad voice |

#### Motif Rules

1. **Fragments before restatements.** In any mix state above ENGAGED, prefer a
   two- or three-note fragment of a motif over its complete statement.
2. **One motif leads at a time.** A layer may reference a second motif as a
   counter-line, but the ear must always be able to name the leading motif.
3. **Motifs are pitched in their own scale.** A combat fragment is never
   transposed into Lydian and a Star Drift fragment never into Phrygian
   Dominant; the scales are the identity.
4. **The motif set is closed.** Adding a fourth motif requires a producer and
   composer decision recorded against this chapter.

### 2.3 Chord Progressions

Retro arcade music often avoids complex harmony, but these progressions provide
structure while staying loop-friendly. All three are modal, diatonic to their
scale, and short enough to loop cleanly at 1–2 bars.

| Progression | Chords | Context / mix state | Tempo zone | Time | Character notes |
|-------------|--------|---------------------|------------|------|-----------------|
| **Combat** | **E – F – E – D** | ENGAGED, ESCALATION | 160 BPM | 4/4 | Minimalist, modal, tense. Works with pulsing bass and rapid arpeggios. The ♭II (F) against the E tonic is the tension generator; the drop to D leaves the phrase hanging and loop-ready. |
| **Combat (boss extension)** | **E – F – G♯ – F – E – D – C – D** | BOSS | 160 BPM | 4/4 | The combat progression stretched to eight bars for boss phases: the G♯ and C add harmonic weight and forward lean without changing the modal identity. |
| **Boss** | **E – C – D – F – E** | BOSS (phase 2 and beyond) | 160 BPM | 4/4 | Chromatic tension; strong "danger escalation" feel; supports heavy FM brass or distorted square leads. |
| **Ambient** | **C – D – E – F♯** | AMBIENT, RESOLVE bed | 80 BPM | 4/4 | Bright, drifting, spacey. Works with pads and slow-moving textures. Can be looped seamlessly or expanded to **C – G – D – F♯** for a longer menu or attract loop. |

#### Tempo Zones and the Fire Grid

AI Hell's player fire is quantised to a silent **80 BPM** beat, and non-weapon
destruction accents are deferred to the next 16th note of the same grid (GDD
§4.4). The music must not fight that grid:

| Tempo zone | BPM | Grid relationship | Used by |
|------------|-----|-------------------|---------|
| **Ambient** | 80 | 1 beat = 750 ms; identical to the fire grid | AMBIENT, RESOLVE |
| **Combat** | 160 | 2 × fire grid; one music beat = two fire-grid beats (375 ms) | ENGAGED, ESCALATION, BOSS |

At 160 BPM the music beat period is exactly 375 ms — the shipped Cannon fire
interval — so an eighth-note pulse in the score aligns with two cannon shots.
Composers should write combat material so that any 16th-note flourish lands on
the same 187.5 ms tick grid the audio accents already use.

#### Composer Note — Loop Architecture

- Every progression is delivered as bar-aligned loops; loop start and loop end
  must land exactly on bar downbeats.
- Atomic loops are 1–2 bars (per the motif brief); stem loops are delivered at
  4 or 8 bars so the vertical mix has room to breathe.
- The ambient progression benefits from a 16-bar loop before the repeat
  becomes perceptible.
- Loop points must be cross-fade compatible with the adjacent mix state, so a
  bar-boundary transition never clips a tail.

## 3. Instrumentation Palette

The AI Hell palette is organised into three functional layers: the **Core
Arcade Ensemble** (always audible), the **Intensity Layer** (introduced
dynamically by the mixing system) and the **Ambience Layer** (always running at
a very low level). The **SFX palette is explicitly out of scope for this
chapter** — the shipped, baked ToneForge catalogue in GDD §7.3 and
[AUDIO_TONEFORGE_CUE_MAPPING.md](AUDIO_TONEFORGE_CUE_MAPPING.md) remains the
single authority for sound effects, and no music asset may reuse or shadow an
SFX cue path.

| Instrument | Role | Layer | Notes and restrictions |
|------------|------|-------|------------------------|
| **FM Bass (2-operator)** | Harmonic anchor; the engine of the score | Core Arcade Ensemble | The defining texture of combat. Rooted on E in combat, C in ambient. Must sit in the 40–120 Hz fundamental range with an FM timbre that stays legible on laptop speakers — never rely on sub-bass alone for the riff. Present in every state. |
| **Square Lead** | Motif carrier; the "voice" of the game | Core Arcade Ensemble | Primary melodic voice. Carries Incoming Wave and Survival Pulse in their primary statements. Duty-cycle variants (12.5 %, 25 %, 50 %) are permitted for timbral shading; pulse-width modulation is permitted but must not smear the motif's attack. |
| **Triangle Arpeggiator** | Motion; harmonic colour at speed | Core Arcade Ensemble | Continuous 16th-note arpeggios in combat states. The arpeggio must follow the current progression's chord tones only; it is the primary density control (see §4), so its note rate and octave span are the first things to scale with intensity. |
| **Noise Percussion** | Pulse; energy; the arcade "heartbeat" | Core Arcade Ensemble | Short noise-channel ticks and bursts in place of a drum kit — hat-like ticks, a short filtered noise snare, and a kick built from a pitched noise sweep. Must never become a modern drum-loop groove; the feel is hardware, not production. |
| **FM Brass (4-operator)** | Weight; threat; boss presence | Intensity Layer | Reserved for BOSS and the top of ESCALATION. Heavy, slightly distorted stabs aligned to the boss progression's chromatic turns. Must enter by crossfade, never by a hard cut. |
| **Distorted Square Sub-Lead** | Edge; aggression | Intensity Layer | A second square voice an octave below the lead, driven harder. Present from ESCALATION upward only. Its job is to make the mix feel "louder" without raising the master level. |
| **Lydian Pad** | Space; scale; relief | Ambience / Core (ambient) | Long-release, slow-attack pad voices carrying the ambient progression. Present in AMBIENT and RESOLVE; must duck to silence before combat material is stated. |
| **Shimmer Arpeggio** | Sparkle; the "starfield" colour | Ambience Layer | A very quiet, high-register triangle arpeggio on the Star Drift contour. Present in AMBIENT only; must be normalised so that it is felt rather than noticed (−30 dBFS target). |
| **Menu Drone** | Continuity; presence | Ambience Layer | A slow, filtered noise-and-pad drone that runs beneath all states. Its function is to prevent the game from ever sounding "switched off". Present in every state at −32 dBFS; never louder. |

### Layer Rules

- **Core always audible.** The Core Arcade Ensemble never disappears entirely;
  a quiet bass and lead must remain so that the mix never drops to nothing
  between states.
- **Intensity Layer is additive only.** Layer instruments may only be added on
  the way up the intensity ramp. Removing them is a transition event (§5), not
  a mix tweak.
- **Ambience is subliminal.** The Ambience Layer is normalised so that its
  content is inaudible in isolation and only perceptible as presence.
- **No acoustic instruments.** The palette is synthetic by design; an acoustic
  instrument appearing in the score would be a genre error, not a colour.

## 4. Dynamic Mixing Rules

AI Hell uses **vertical remixing**: rather than transitioning between
different pieces of music, the score is authored as a set of parallel stems
(individual instrument tracks) that are faded in and out based on the current
game state. All stems within a tempo zone are rendered bar-aligned and at the
same length, so the engine can change the emotional intensity of the music
without ever playing a different piece.

Five named mix states govern the full session lifecycle.

| State | Active stems | Volume levels | Trigger conditions | Max dwell |
|-------|--------------|---------------|--------------------|-----------|
| **AMBIENT** | Lydian Pad; Star Drift shimmer arpeggio; FM Bass (C); Menu Drone | Pad −8 dB • Shimmer −18 dB • Bass −14 dB • Lead silent • Arp silent • Noise perc silent • Drone −32 dB | Main menu; attract/demo mode; between-run screens; no active wave | Unlimited (loop indefinitely) |
| **ENGAGED** | FM Bass (E); Square Lead; Triangle Arp (16ths); Noise Percussion; Menu Drone | Bass −3 dB • Lead −6 dB • Arp −9 dB • Perc −10 dB • Drone −32 dB | A wave is active and the player is alive | Duration of the wave; no ceiling |
| **ESCALATION** | ENGAGED stems + Distorted Square Sub-Lead + FM Brass (stabs) | Bass −1 dB • Lead −4 dB • Arp −7 dB • Perc −8 dB • Sub-lead −10 dB • Brass −9 dB • Drone −34 dB | Lives ≤ 1; **or** live enemy count ≥ the level's `escalationEnemyThreshold`; **or** wave elapsed ≥ 75 % with ≥ 25 % of the wave's enemies alive; **or** boss health ≤ 25 % | While the escalation trigger holds; reverts to ENGAGED after a 4-bar hold once the trigger clears (see §5.2) |
| **BOSS** | All Core stems + Intensity Layer + boss brass; Menu Drone | Bass 0 dB • Lead −3 dB • Arp −6 dB • Perc −7 dB • Sub-lead −8 dB • Brass −4 dB • Drone −34 dB | Boss encounter active | The boss encounter; no ceiling while a phase is live |
| **RESOLVE** | Ambient stems only (Lydian Pad, Bass, Drone) plus a one-shot resolution stinger | Pad −10 dB • Bass −16 dB • Drone −32 dB • every combat stem silent | Run ended (victory or defeat) and the game-over screen is showing | The game-over screen; returns to AMBIENT when the player leaves it |

### Mixing Rules

1. **Bar-boundary commits.** Every stem-level change commits on the next bar
   boundary of the current tempo zone, except the boss-arrival cut-in (§5.1).
2. **No direct ESCALATION → AMBIENT.** If escalation conditions vanish (a
   wave is cleared, a life is restored), the mix must step down through
   ENGAGED and hold ENGAGED for at least four bars. A direct collapse from
   ESCALATION to AMBIENT would read as a bug to the player.
3. **Choice-overlay duck.** While the mineral-choice or upgrade-choice overlay
   is open — including the shipped 3-2-1 countdown — the music bus ducks by
   **−12 dB** over one bar and stays ducked until gameplay resumes. The
   countdown ticks in GDD §7.3 remain the audible foreground; music must not
   compete with them.
4. **Death duck.** On player death the music ducks to −18 dB over 0.5 s and
   holds while the death sequence plays; the RESOLVE state then fades in.
5. **Pause duck.** On pause, blur or loss of focus the music bus ducks to
   −12 dB over one bar; resume restores the previous state over two bars.
6. **Never mask a telegraph.** Music must remain at least 6 dB below any
   in-flight advance cue (the ≥ 500 ms warning cues in GDD §7.3). If a mix
   state would mask one, the mix state is wrong.
7. **No new SFX.** The mixer adds no sound effects; SFX remain the baked
   ToneForge assets of GDD §7.3.

## 5. State-Driven Music Transitions

### 5.1 Transition Design Principles

The guiding principle for all AI Hell music transitions is **perceptual
invisibility**. A player who is not listening for musical changes should never
perceive a transition as mechanical.

- **Bar-boundary synchronisation.** All stem-level transitions initiate on the
  next musical bar boundary following the trigger event. The game signals the
  audio system at the moment of the trigger; the audio system queues the
  transition to the next downbeat. This eliminates rhythmic dislocation.
- **Maximum transition window.** No transition may take longer than **two
  bars** to complete; every fade must reach its target within that window.
- **Crossfade only.** Stems enter and exit by crossfade. Abrupt mutes and
  unmutes are forbidden, with the single exception of the boss-arrival
  cut-in below.
- **The Unaware Player Rule.** Before a transition is signed off, it must be
  playtested by someone who is not working on audio. If they notice the change
  as a change, it must be revised.
- **Boss-cut-in exception.** Boss arrivals are the only hard cut-in: the boss
  stinger fires immediately on trigger, while the adaptive stems continue
  their bar cycle underneath, so the stinger momentarily dominates the mix.
- **Grid alignment.** Where a stinger does not cut in, it quantises to the next
  16th-note tick of the shared 80 BPM beat clock (187.5 ms), matching the
  shipped explosion-accent rule so musical and gameplay accents share one grid.

### 5.2 Transition Matrix

| From | To | Trigger | Method | Duration |
|------|----|---------|--------|----------|
| AMBIENT | ENGAGED | Wave 1 spawns; player gains control of the ship | Crossfade: bass moves C → E, arp and noise percussion fade in, pad fades out, shimmer arpeggio fades out over two bars | 2 bars |
| ENGAGED | ESCALATION | Any ESCALATION trigger in §4 becomes true on a bar boundary | Crossfade: sub-lead and brass stabs fade in; arp rate may double; bass and lead levels step up | 1–2 bars (minimum one full bar) |
| ESCALATION | BOSS | Boss encounter starts | Boss cut-in stinger fires immediately; stems crossfade from the combat progression to the boss progression on the next bar | Immediate (stinger); 2 bars (stems) |
| ESCALATION | ENGAGED | Escalation trigger clears (life restored, wave thinned) | Crossfade out sub-lead and brass over two bars; arp rate returns; **4-bar ENGAGED hold** before any further downward transition | 2 bars + 4-bar hold |
| BOSS | ESCALATION | Boss phase ends but the encounter continues at reduced pressure | Crossfade: boss brass steps back, combat progression returns | 2 bars |
| BOSS | RESOLVE | Boss destroyed (victory) | Combat stems fade over two bars; the victory resolution stinger fires on the bar after the boss death frame; ambient bed fades in | 2 bars (stems); stinger immediate |
| ANY | RESOLVE | Player death | Duck to −18 dB over 0.5 s, then crossfade to ambient bed; the defeat resolution stinger fires on entry | 0.5 s (duck); 2 bars (bed) |
| RESOLVE | AMBIENT | Player leaves the game-over screen (menu, replay, attract) | Pad and drone continue; combat stems remain silent; no restatement needed | 2 bars |
| ANY | (ducked) | Choice overlay opens (mineral/upgrade, including 3-2-1 countdown) | Music bus ducks to −12 dB over one bar | 1 bar |
| (ducked) | ANY | Choice overlay closes and gameplay resumes | Music bus restores over one bar; any state change is queued to the next bar boundary | 1 bar |
| ANY | (ducked) | Pause, blur or loss of focus | Music bus ducks to −12 dB over one bar; state is held | 1 bar |

### 5.3 Stinger System

Stingers are short, one-shot musical events that fire over the adaptive score
without interrupting it. They are delivered as discrete one-shot assets, mixed
as a single stereo file, with a 250 ms silence tail to prevent click artefacts
on completion. Every stinger is built from the chapter's motifs and is
harmonically compatible with every mix state it can fire in.

| Stinger | Duration | Instrumentation | Trigger | Harmonic notes |
|---------|----------|-----------------|---------|----------------|
| **Wave Incoming** | 1 bar (≈ 1.5 s at 160 BPM) | Square lead states Incoming Wave (E–F–G♯–F–E–D) over a bass E pedal; one noise-percussion hit on the downbeat | Wave spawn, once per wave, aligned to the wave-spawn advance cue in GDD §7.3 | Fully diatonic to E Phrygian Dominant; safe over the combat progression. Must not play over the boss intro. |
| **Boss Arrival** | 2 bars (≈ 3 s at 160 BPM) | Low FM brass on the boss progression's E–C turn, noise swell, and a rising square-lead fragment E–F–G♯ | Boss spawn; fires as an immediate cut-in (no bar wait) | Uses only E, F, G, G♯, C, D tones that are common to the boss progression; the swell must not state a simultaneous F♯ against an F. |
| **Boss Phase Shift** | 1 bar (≈ 1.5 s) | FM brass stab plus a distorted square rise on G♯–A–B | Boss phase transition, aligned to the shipped rising-tone advance cue | Diatonic to E Phrygian Dominant; reinforces the phase change without a key change. |
| **Last Life** | 1.5 s | Survival Pulse fragment (G♯–A–G♯–F) on a single square lead, undoubled, over the drone | First frame on which the player's lives drop to 1 | Motivic and harmonically neutral; must fire only once per life loss, never looping with the ESCALATION bed. |
| **Victory** | 4 bars (≈ 6 s at 80 BPM) | Square lead statement of Incoming Wave resolving upward, supported by triangle arpeggio on the ambient progression's C–D–E, ending on a sustained E | Boss destroyed | Resolves inside the established language — a bright, earned landing on E, not a modulation to a major key. |
| **Defeat** | 2 bars (≈ 6 s at 80 BPM) | Pitch-falling FM bass glissando from E down to A, one square-lead fragment (D–C), fading drone | Player death, fires on entry into RESOLVE | Uses the flat seventh (D) and flat sixth (C) for descent; no bright resolution, no comedic sting. |

#### Stinger Suppression Rules

- **No stacking.** A stinger suppression window of **2 seconds** follows any
  stinger fire; no further stinger may trigger inside it.
- **Priority order** when triggers coincide:
  Boss Arrival > Defeat > Victory > Boss Phase Shift > Last Life > Wave
  Incoming.
- **Advance cues win.** If a stinger would overlap an in-flight GDD §7.3
  advance cue (≥ 500 ms telegraph), the stinger is delayed to the next beat
  rather than masking the cue.
- **One-shot only.** Stingers never loop and never carry stem-level mix
  automation; if a stinger needs a mix change, that is a transition (§5.2).

## 6. Implementation Guidelines

### 6.1 Audio Middleware

AI Hell ships to the **web** and to **Windows** (GDD §6.3) on a Phaser/Web
Audio stack, and the shipped SFX pipeline is a deterministic build-time bake
(see GDD §7.3). The middleware guidance therefore follows the existing
architecture rather than importing a new dependency:

| Option | Status | Rationale |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| **In-engine Web Audio stem mixer** | **Primary recommendation** | Zero new dependencies; works on web and in the packaged Windows build; the game already owns an `AudioContext` and a manifest-driven playback layer in `src/audio/effects.ts`. Stems are baked WAV assets fetched from the game's own static audio directory, so music reuses the shipped bake-and-checksum convention. |
| **FMOD Studio** | Secondary | Considerable strength for authored transitions and DSP, and a good fit if a future native (non-browser) build needs platform audio features. Adds a native dependency and an authoring pipeline that the current web-first build does not have. |
| **Wwise** | Tertiary | Equivalent capability to FMOD via RTPC and switch containers; only worth adopting if the team already holds Wwise expertise or licensing. Its event naming would map from §6.2 without changing any musical intent. |

**Decision gate.** The middleware decision must be confirmed by the audio
programmer with the lead engineer **before** any stem delivery begins, because
stem format, loop-point metadata and bank layout differ between options. The
musical content in §1–§5 is middleware-agnostic and remains valid either way.

**Bake and determinism.** Whichever transport is chosen, music assets follow
the shipped convention: pre-rendered, committed to the repository (or to a
documented asset path), verified by SHA-256 checksums, and reproducible from a
pinned toolchain. Music must never be synthesised at runtime as a hidden
side-effect of gameplay.

### 6.2 Music Event Architecture

All music events live under a `music/` namespace so they can never collide with
the `sfx/` catalogue of GDD §7.3.

| Event path | Type | Description |
|------------|------|-------------|
| `music/main_adaptive` | Adaptive (looping) | The single adaptive event containing every music stem across all mix states. Driven by the `GameState` parameter. Started once at session start and never stopped and restarted to change state — parameter changes only. |
| `music/state/ambient` | State marker | Logical marker for the AMBIENT stem set (implemented as `GameState = 0` on `music/main_adaptive`). |
| `music/state/engaged` | State marker | ENGAGED stem set (`GameState = 1`). |
| `music/state/escalation` | State marker | ESCALATION stem set (`GameState = 2`). |
| `music/state/boss` | State marker | BOSS stem set (`GameState = 3`). |
| `music/state/resolve` | State marker | RESOLVE stem set (`GameState = 4`). |
| `music/stingers/wave_incoming` | One-shot | Wave Incoming stinger. |
| `music/stingers/boss_arrival` | One-shot | Boss Arrival stinger (cut-in). |
| `music/stingers/boss_phase` | One-shot | Boss Phase Shift stinger. |
| `music/stingers/last_life` | One-shot | Last Life stinger. |
| `music/stingers/victory` | One-shot | Victory resolution stinger. |
| `music/stingers/defeat` | One-shot | Defeat resolution stinger. |
| `music/ambience/menu_drone` | Persistent (looping) | The always-on Menu Drone bed; started at session start, stopped at session end. |

#### Motif Object Model

Motifs are stored as atomic objects rather than as rendered audio:

- an **interval set** (semitone offsets from the key centre), and
- a **rhythm cell** (note lengths as beat fractions, tied to the tempo zone).

A motif instance is then `(motif, transposition, rhythm cell, fragment range,
layer, mix state)`, which makes recombination, fragmentation and density
scaling data operations rather than re-recording sessions. Any tooling that
ships a motif must expose this object shape so the docs guard and the
implementation contract can trace each entry back to §2.2.

### 6.3 Parameter Mapping Table

| Parameter | Range | Driven by | Affects |
|-----------|-------|-----------|---------|
| `GameState` | 0 (AMBIENT) → 4 (RESOLVE), integer | The game-state machine, updated on every state change | Primary stem levels on `music/main_adaptive`; tempo zone selection (80 or 160 BPM); which stinger family is legal |
| `WaveProgress` | 0.0 → 1.0, continuous | Wave logic: elapsed wave time ÷ wave duration | ESCALATION trigger evaluation; triangle-arpeggio octave span within ENGAGED (wider as the wave progresses) |
| `LivesRemaining` | 0 → 3, integer | Player state | Last Life stinger trigger (first frame at 1); ESCALATION trigger; lead/brass level trim |
| `EnemyDensity` | 0.0 → 1.0, continuous | Live enemy count ÷ the level's `escalationEnemyThreshold`, clamped | ESCALATION trigger; noise-percussion density; arpeggio note rate |
| `BossPhase` | 0 (no boss) → 4 | Boss state machine | Boss progression selection (combat extension vs boss chromatic); Boss Phase Shift stinger; brass level |
| `BeatPhase` | 0.0 → 1.0, continuous, from the shared 80 BPM beat clock | The shared beat clock (`src/utils/beat.ts`) | Transition and stinger quantisation (next bar boundary, next 16th-note tick) — the single clock shared with player fire and explosion accents |
| `MusicDuck` | 0.0 (full level) → 1.0 (ducked), continuous | Choice overlay, pause/blur, death sequence | Music-bus gain offset (0 dB → −12 dB choice/pause, 0 dB → −18 dB death) |

### 6.4 Technical Specifications

**Scope note.** These specifications cover **music assets only**. Sound-effect
specifications — sample rate, peak normalisation, variation counts and cue
names — remain exactly as documented in GDD §7.3 and
[AUDIO_TONEFORGE_CUE_MAPPING.md](AUDIO_TONEFORGE_CUE_MAPPING.md).

**Music stems**

- Sample rate 44.1 kHz; 24-bit WAV; stereo (mono is acceptable for a solo
  instrument where the mono image is preferable).
- All stems within a mix state must share an identical bar count and length.
- Loop start and loop end fall exactly on bar downbeats, verified in the DAW
  before delivery.
- Four beats of silence pre-roll are retained in the file for engine alignment.
- Integrated loudness target −14 LUFS per mix state, with a true peak ceiling
  of −1 dBTP. Peak-normalise stingers to −1 dBFS.

**Ambience loops**

- Minimum four minutes before a perceptible repeat; eight minutes preferred for
  the Menu Drone.
- A two-second crossfade is baked into the loop point to remove any seam.
- Delivered at 44.1 kHz / 24-bit; compressed in the build for distribution.

**Stingers**

- 250 ms of silence appended to the end of every stinger file.
- Delivered as a single pre-mixed stereo file (no stems).
- Quantised to the next 16th-note tick of the 80 BPM beat clock, except the
  Boss Arrival cut-in.

**Gain staging**

- Music bus sits under the SFX bus so that no music state can mask an advance
  cue (see §4, rule 6).
- Combat stems are mixed so that the lead is intelligible on laptop speakers:
  critical content lives in the 200 Hz – 5 kHz band, with sub-bass as
  reinforcement rather than as the carrier.

### 6.5 Audio Budget Table

| Platform | Max simultaneous voices | Music stems | Ambience layers | Compressed asset budget |
|----------|-------------------------|-------------|-----------------|-------------------------|
| **Windows desktop (primary target)** | 32 | Up to 8 stems (all layers at BOSS/ESCALATION) | 2 (Menu Drone, Shimmer Arpeggio) | ≈ 6–10 MB per mix-state family at 128 kbps Vorbis |
| **Desktop browser (Chrome/Edge)** | 24 | Up to 8 stems | 2 | ≈ 6–10 MB, streamed on demand |
| **Low-power laptop / integrated GPU** | 16 | Up to 5 stems; BOSS collapses to bass, lead, arp, percussion and one intensity stem | 1 (Menu Drone only; Shimmer Arpeggio suppressed) | ≈ 4–6 MB |

**Delivery note.** Stems are fetched lazily per tempo zone; the ambient set is
loaded with the menu, the combat set when a run starts. A missing stem must
degrade to silence, never to a stall, and must never fetch a fallback SFX cue.

## 7. Composer and Sound Designer Briefs

### 7.1 Composer Brief

**Overview.** You are composing the adaptive score for AI Hell: a neon,
retro-arcade bullet-hell survival game. The score must carry the player through
ten escalating levels with a single coherent musical identity — Phrygian
Dominant for combat, Lydian for the space between — built from three short
motifs that survive fragmentation, recombination and constant layering.

**Deliverables**

| Deliverable | Specification | Notes |
|-------------|---------------|-------|
| **AMBIENT stems** | 16-bar loop; Lydian Pad, FM Bass (C), Shimmer Arpeggio; 80 BPM; ambient progression C–D–E–F♯ (expanded C–G–D–F♯ for menu) | Loop seamlessly, bar-aligned, separate stems. Star Drift must be present as the membrane of this state. |
| **ENGAGED stems** | 8-bar loop; bass, square lead, triangle arp (16ths), noise percussion; 160 BPM; combat progression E–F–E–D | Deliver each instrument as a separate stem. Lead states Incoming Wave, then Survival Pulse as the bed. |
| **ESCALATION stems** | 8-bar loop; ENGAGED stems plus distorted sub-lead and brass stabs; 160 BPM; combat progression with Survival Pulse doubling | The escalation is felt as density and weight, not as a new tune. |
| **BOSS stems** | 8-bar loop; full ensemble plus FM brass; 160 BPM; boss progression E–C–D–F–E and the eight-bar combat extension | The emotional ceiling of the score. Deliver all stems separately. |
| **RESOLVE stems** | 8-bar loop; ambient bed only; 80 BPM; ambient progression | No combat residue. The resolution stingers carry the outcome. |
| **Stingers** | Six files: Wave Incoming, Boss Arrival, Boss Phase Shift, Last Life, Victory, Defeat. 250 ms silence tail. | Single pre-mixed stereo files; no stems. See §5.3 for duration and harmonic constraints. |
| **Attract loop** | 90 s standalone AMBIENT piece for the menu and attract/demo mode | Must hand over cleanly to `music/state/ambient`. |

**Reference mood vocabulary.** hostile, geometric, relentless, arcade, hypnotic,
cold-bright, propulsive, mechanical, menacing, weightless, neon, inevitable.

**Forbidden elements**

- No acoustic or orchestral instruments; the palette is FM and chiptune
  waveforms only.
- No key change out of the two documented scales, and no major-key "reward"
  ending.
- No motif longer than six notes, and no new motif beyond the documented three
  without a recorded design decision.
- No wall-to-wall arrangement: density is scaled, not saturated, and silence
  between phrases is a compositional tool.
- No music over the choice countdown beyond the ducked bed — the countdown
  ticks must stay the foreground.
- No tempo outside the documented zones; the music must never fight the 80 BPM
  fire grid.

### 7.2 Sound Designer Brief

**Scope.** This chapter adds **no new sound effects**. SFX are, and remain, the
deterministic baked ToneForge catalogue specified in GDD §7.3, with the
authoritative cue-to-recipe mapping in
[AUDIO_TONEFORGE_CUE_MAPPING.md](AUDIO_TONEFORGE_CUE_MAPPING.md). The sound
designer's work for the music system is therefore integration and gain
staging, not new cue creation.

**Responsibilities**

- **Gain staging against the SFX bus.** Verify in-game that every music state
  leaves each advance telegraph cue (≥ 500 ms lead, GDD §7.3) clearly audible;
  music must sit at least 6 dB below an in-flight cue.
- **Bake and checksum.** Take delivery of composer stems, bake them through the
  project's deterministic asset pipeline, and record SHA-256 checksums
  alongside the assets so a rebuild is verifiable.
- **Loop metadata.** Verify that every delivered loop point lands on a bar
  downbeat and that adjacent mix states cross-fade without clipping a tail.
- **Audition matrix.** Build the audition sheet: for each mix state, the stem
  set, the level table, and the transitions in and out. Sign-off requires
  auditioning every state and every transition at least once on laptop
  speakers and once on headphones.
- **Silence audit.** Confirm the mixer never leaves the game silent by
  accident (the Menu Drone guarantees presence) and never leaves a stale stem
  audible after a transition.
- **Accessibility hand-off.** Confirm that the shipped visual telegraphs remain
  the primary carriers of every gameplay-critical cue, so that music — like
  SFX — is never the sole channel for information (see §8).

## 8. Audio Accessibility

The music system must be accessible across hearing abilities, hardware and
listening environments, and it must not weaken the accessibility guarantees the
shipped SFX already provide.

### Volume Controls

Four independent sliders, each 0–100 %, persisted across sessions:

| Slider | Controls | Default |
|--------|----------|---------|
| **Master** | Overall output; scales all other sliders | 80 % |
| **Music** | `music/main_adaptive`, all stingers, the attract loop | 70 % |
| **SFX** | Every cue in the GDD §7.3 catalogue | 85 % |
| **Ambience** | `music/ambience/menu_drone` and the ambient bed | 60 % |

A **Music: Off** setting is equivalent to Music 0 % and must not affect SFX,
countdown ticks or telegraph cues.

### Visual Companions

Every musical event that carries gameplay meaning must have a visual companion
that fires on the same frame. This mirrors the shipped rule for SFX and is
mandatory:

| Musical event | Visual companion |
|---------------|------------------|
| Wave Incoming stinger | Wave banner / formation spawn animation |
| Boss Arrival stinger | Boss entrance animation and health bar reveal |
| Boss Phase Shift stinger | Phase colour change and phase indicator |
| Last Life stinger | Lives HUD change plus a brief warning flash |
| ESCALATION bed | Subtle HUD intensity cue (e.g. a score-tension or threat indicator) |
| Victory / Defeat stinger | Full result screen with explicit outcome text |

### No Audio-Only Information

This is an absolute constraint, unchanged from the shipped MVP: **no
game-critical information may be communicated by audio alone.** Music is
atmosphere and pressure only; it must never be the only signal that a wave has
started, that a boss phase has changed, that a life was lost, or that a run has
ended. Where a musical event and a gameplay event coincide, the gameplay event
must already be visually legible.

### Listening Contexts

- **Mono compatibility.** Every stem and stinger must be checked in mono; no
  critical content may be lost or phase-cancelled. Critical material lives in
  the centre of the stereo field.
- **Laptop and small speakers.** Sub-bass is reinforcement only; the lead,
  arpeggio and percussion must remain legible without low-frequency response.
- **Reduced-range option.** A settings toggle lowers the noise-percussion and
  shimmer-arpeggio high-frequency content for listeners sensitive to bright,
  harsh material.
- **No sudden level spikes.** Stinger loudness is normalised so that a stinger
  never exceeds the mix by more than 6 dB, avoiding startling jumps in level.

## Appendix B — Glossary

Plain-language definitions for team members who are not audio specialists.
Terms are alphabetical.

| Term | Plain-language definition |
|------|---------------------------|
| **Arpeggio** | A chord played one note at a time rather than all at once. Fast arpeggios are how AI Hell adds speed and density without adding new melodies. |
| **Bar boundary** | The exact moment a new musical bar begins — the downbeat. In AI Hell, stem transitions only happen on bar boundaries so the rhythm never sounds disrupted, like waiting for a natural pause before changing the subject. |
| **Beat grid (80 BPM)** | The game's internal, inaudible pulse. Player fire is quantised to it and destruction accents are delayed to its 16th notes (GDD §4.4). Music tempo zones are multiples of it so that musical and gameplay accents land together. |
| **BPM (beats per minute)** | How fast the music moves. 80 BPM means one beat every 750 ms; 160 BPM means one every 375 ms. AI Hell uses 80 BPM for ambient states and 160 BPM for combat — exactly double, so the two share one grid. |
| **Chiptune waveform** | The basic tones of early game hardware — square, triangle and noise. AI Hell's palette is built from these plus FM synthesis. |
| **Crossfade** | One layer fades out while another fades in, so there is never a gap or a jump. AI Hell uses crossfades for every musical change. |
| **Ducking** | Temporarily lowering the music so that something more important (a warning cue, a countdown tick) can be heard. The choice overlay and pause both duck the music bus. |
| **FM synthesis** | Frequency modulation synthesis: one oscillator bends another to create bright, metallic or bell-like tones. Two operators give the classic arcade bass; four give the heavier boss brass. |
| **Loop point** | The exact position where a piece jumps back to its start so it can repeat without a seam. AI Hell loops are aligned to bar boundaries and delivered with bar-aligned metadata. |
| **Lydian mode** | A scale with a raised fourth degree, which sounds bright, floating and "cosmic". In C, the notes are C – D – E – F♯ – G – A – B. AI Hell's ambient voice. |
| **Phrygian Dominant mode** | A scale with a flattened second degree, giving it an exotic, threatening character. In E, the notes are E – F – G♯ – A – B – C – D. AI Hell's combat voice. |
| **Stem** | A single instrument track delivered as its own file, so the game can fade it independently. The whole AI Hell score is built from stems so intensity can change without changing the piece. |
| **Stinger** | A short, pre-composed cue that fires once on top of the looping score for a dramatic moment — a wave starting, a boss arriving, a life lost, a run ending. |
| **Tempo zone** | A documented speed band (80 or 160 BPM in AI Hell) that all stems within a mix state share, so parallel playback never drifts. |
| **Vertical remixing** | Playing several layers of the same music at once and changing the volume of each layer, rather than switching between different pieces. It is AI Hell's core intensity technique. |
